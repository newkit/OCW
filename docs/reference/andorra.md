What we took from them (spec 4.6.1 table 6, `assets/building-styles.toml` style `pyrenean`):

- **Houses:** grey and brown rubble stone (70 % of the walls outside the
  towns' plastered upper floors), dark slate roofs (90 %), low gables
  (pitch 0.7) with wide eaves, timber balconies along the eaves; the
  apartment blocks of the capital are stone-clad below, plaster above.
- **Forests:** pine forests, not spruce: Scots pine in the valleys with some
  fir on the north slopes, the upright mountain pine above 1,700 m up to
  the tree line at about 2,300 – 2,400 m; birches, ashes and oaks only
  along the rivers and on the lowest slopes; above the tree line pasture,
  juniper, rhododendron and scree.
- **Ground:** granite and dark schist; the road edges carry pine needles and
  cones, the high roads rocks and dwarf shrubs (`ground_detail`).
- **Not drawn yet:** the upright mountain pine as its own model (it is drawn
  as a Scots pine below the krummholz belt), the holm oak (drawn with the
  evergreen laurel model), granite and schist as their own rock materials
  (the default limestone of the library), stone walls along the hay
  meadows, the snow poles of the passes.

No open orthophoto covers Andorra (PNOA and the ICGC's stop at the border,
the Govern d'Andorra's flights have no open licence), so there are no
orthophoto crops on this board: the region look comes from the Sentinel-2
seasonal composites alone.
